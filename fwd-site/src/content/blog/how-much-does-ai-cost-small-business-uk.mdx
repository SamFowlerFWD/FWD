Ask ten people what AI costs a business and you'll get answers from "it's free" to "budget six figures". They're all right, because "using AI" covers everything from one person with a ChatGPT login to a bespoke system reading thousands of documents a day.

Most small businesses need the cheap end of that range, used properly. This guide breaks down what each level costs, what you get for it, and where I see money wasted.

## The Short Answer

| What | Typical Cost | Good For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) | Free, or around £20/month per person | Writing, summarising, research, ideas |
| Training | £299-£499 with me, £1,000+ from larger training firms | Getting your team using those tools properly |
| AI inside an automation | From £799 to build, plus small running costs | Taking a repetitive job off someone's plate |
| AI features in a custom app | Part of the app build, from £1,299 | AI working inside your own systems |
| AI consultancy | A few hundred to well over £1,000 a day | Strategy for larger organisations |

Most of my clients start at the top of that table and work down, one step at a time. That's the right way round.

## Level 1: Off-the-Shelf AI Tools

ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft Copilot all have free versions, and paid plans for individuals cost around £20 a month. Business and team plans cost a bit more per person. Mostly you're paying for admin controls and an agreement that your business data won't be used to train their models.

For drafting emails, summarising long documents, writing first drafts of web copy, or thinking through a tricky problem, these tools are brilliant value. There's no reason to spend more until you've got real use out of them.

**The hidden cost is time.** Most people try AI, ask it something vague, get a vague answer back, and decide it's overhyped. Then the subscription sits unused. The tool wasn't the problem.

## Level 2: Training

This is the cheapest way to make the subscription worth paying for. Good training isn't about clever prompts. It's about working out which of *your* tasks AI is good at, which it's bad at, and how to check its work.

My [AI training](/services/ai-training) costs £299 for a two-hour 1-to-1 session or £499 for a half-day team workshop of up to 8 people, built around your actual work rather than generic examples. Larger training firms typically charge £1,000 or more for a half day.

If you just want the basics, it's also worth checking your local council and growth hub. Some run free or subsidised introductory sessions for small businesses.

**Good for:** any business already paying for AI tools and not getting much out of them.

## Level 3: AI Inside Your Automations

This is where AI stops being something you chat to and starts doing jobs in the background. A few examples I see a lot:

- Reading incoming enquiries, sorting them by type, and drafting a reply for you to check
- Pulling the details out of invoices, receipts or forms and putting them in the right system
- Checking uploaded documents. The [Horsebox Hire platform](/blog/building-horsebox-hire-software) uses AI to read driving licences and utility bills and flag anything that looks wrong
- Turning rough notes into a tidy report, summary or customer email

**Build cost: from £799** for one or two simple automations, up to around £3,000 when several systems are involved. My [automation service](/services/business-process-automation) has the detail, and [5 business tasks you should automate](/blog/business-tasks-you-should-automate) has costs for specific jobs.

**Running cost: usually a few pounds a month.** You pay the AI provider per use rather than per person, and for a typical small business the volumes are small. I'll estimate it before we start so there are no surprises.

## Level 4: AI Features in Your Own Software

When AI is built into a custom app or platform, it can work with your own data in ways a chat window never could.

On [Hoofflix](/blog/building-hoofflix), the professional platform I built for farriers, members can search more than 8,600 research papers by meaning rather than keywords. The founder, nearly 70 and a self-confessed technophobe, talks his articles and courses through with his AI assistant and they land in the platform as drafts. Nothing goes live until a person presses Publish, so he can't break anything.

That last part is the important bit. AI in your systems should always have guardrails built into the software itself, not just instructions asking it nicely to behave.

**Cost:** part of the [app build](/services/custom-app-development). Adding an AI feature to an app might be a few hundred pounds of work, or a big chunk of the project, depending on what it needs to do. See [how much an app costs](/blog/how-much-does-an-app-cost-uk) for the wider picture.

## Where Money Gets Wasted

**Licences nobody uses.** Buying AI seats for the whole team before anyone knows what they'll use them for. Start with two or three keen people, then expand.

**Chatbots that annoy customers.** A website chatbot that can't actually answer questions, or makes things up, does more harm than a contact form. If you want one, it needs to be properly connected to your real information, with an easy route to a human.

**Building custom when a subscription would do.** If a £20 a month tool does the job, use the tool. Custom work makes sense when you need AI working inside your own processes, at volume, or with your own data.

**Strategy with nothing built.** A 40-page AI strategy document doesn't save anyone any time. For a small business, the best strategy is usually: pick the most annoying repetitive job, fix it, and see what you learn.

## A Word on Data

Under UK GDPR you're responsible for what happens to your customers' personal data, including what you paste into an AI tool. As a rule of thumb, don't put customer names, addresses or anything sensitive into free AI tools. Use business plans that don't train on your data, and if AI is built into your systems, make sure you know where the data goes and that it stays protected. This is something I cover in every training session and design into every build.

## Where to Start

1. **Pick one tool and use it properly for a month.** ChatGPT or Claude, on real work.
2. **Get some training** if it isn't clicking, or if you want the whole team using it the same way.
3. **Automate one painful job.** The one that makes you sigh every Monday morning.
4. **Then decide** whether AI needs to go deeper into your systems.
