Plenty of yards still run on a whiteboard in the tack room, a yard diary and a WhatsApp group that never goes quiet. It works, right up until someone's extra turnout doesn't get invoiced, or two people both think they've booked the school at six.

There's decent software for this now, and some of it is cheap.

Before I compare anything, I want to be upfront. I build software, and I have **not** built a livery system for a yard. I'm a farrier too, so I've stood in plenty of yards, and I've built things that are close (more on those further down). But this isn't a "buy from me instead" post. For most yards, one of the products below is the right answer.

I've picked five that are live, built for UK yards and riding schools, and publish their prices. All prices were checked on the vendors' own pages in November 2026. They change, so check before you sign up.

## Two different jobs

It helps to split this in two.

**Livery yard management** is about horses and owners: records, vet and farrier reminders, extras like turnout or rugging, staff jobs, and turning all of that into an invoice at the end of the month.

**Riding school booking** is about riders and lessons: a booking calendar, payment up front, rider records and the horses doing the work.

Some products try to do both. Most are clearly better at one.

## LIVERYLive

**What it does well:** turning owner requests into jobs and invoices. Owners ask for extras in the app, staff accept them, and once the job's done it goes on the owner's bill. It also covers horse records (health, vaccinations, worming, feed and turnout groups), staff rotas, yard documents with "marked as read" tracking, and card payments through Stripe, and it syncs with Xero or QuickBooks. Arena bookings are included too, for liveries and outside riders, paid online.

**Who it suits:** full, part, DIY or mixed livery yards, especially where extras are where the money leaks out.

**Price:** £2 per horse per month, with unlimited staff. Owners use the app free. Their own example is a 25-horse yard at £50 a month. There's a six-week free trial and no long-term contract. See [LIVERYLive pricing](https://liverylive.com/pricing/).

## At The Yard

**What it does well:** letting the liveries do the ordering. Owners order services and products, and book facilities like the sand school, from their own account. Every billable job is recorded as it happens and invoiced automatically. It also does stock control, staff work lists and a horse information database, and it links to Xero.

**Who it suits:** livery yards that sell a lot of extras and products, like bedding and feed, and want fewer "what's this charge?" questions.

**Price:** set by yard size. £30 a month for up to 10 horses, £50 for up to 20, £70 for up to 30 and £90 for up to 40, with bigger yards asked to get in touch. The two larger packages include a public shop for selling services, renting out facilities and offering temporary stabling, which is a £10 a month add-on on the smaller ones. There's a 60-day free trial. See [At The Yard's pricing](https://www.attheyard.co.uk/).

## Horsepower

**What it does well:** the day-to-day running of the yard. Horse records, vet and farrier reminders, a shared yard calendar, feed plans, fields and turnout, with native iPhone and Android apps. Livery invoices can be sent from the app or drafted into Xero or QuickBooks, and invited members are free on every plan, so you're not paying per livery.

**Who it suits:** smaller and private yards, and livery yards where the main headache is keeping everyone on the same page rather than the billing.

**Price:** free for one yard with up to 3 horses, £9.99 a month for up to 20 horses, and £19.99 a month for unlimited yards and horses. They say your records stay readable if you cancel, which is a question worth asking every vendor. See [Horsepower's plans](https://horsepower.page/plans).

## EC Pro

**What it does well:** online booking for riding centres, with payment taken up front. It covers bookings, reporting, welfare records for inspections, staff communication and membership packages, and comes with custom branding and an optional website.

**Who it suits:** riding schools and equestrian centres where lessons and activities are the business.

**Price:** three tiers, all plus VAT, each with a booking fee that's charged to your customer at checkout, once per basket, rather than to you. £155 a month with a £1.20 booking fee, £80 a month with a £2.40 fee, or nothing a month with a £3.60 fee. No setup fees, a 30-day rolling contract, a free three-month trial, and an extra 20% off for BHS approved centres. See [EC Pro's UK pricing](https://ecpro.co.uk/pricing/uk).

The booking fee model is worth a moment's thought. On the free tier, a rider booking a lesson pays £3.60 on top. Whether your riders will mind is something only you can judge.

## Pegasys (ABRS+)

**What it does well:** an all-in-one system for riding centres at one flat price. Bookings and a recurring calendar, rider and horse records with vet, farrier and vaccination reminders, staff management, invoices and recurring billing with Stripe payments, and credit packs.

**Who it suits:** riding schools that are members of the Association of British Riding Schools, or happy to join. It's only available to ABRS+ members.

**Price:** £45 a month, flat. It's been available to members since April 2026. See [Pegasys from ABRS+](https://www.abrs-info.org/pegasys/).

## Questions worth asking any of them

- Can I export my data, and what happens to it if I stop paying?
- Am I paying per horse, per yard or per person, and which of those gets expensive as I grow?
- Does the app work on the phone signal I actually get at the yard?
- Can owners pay by card or direct debit, or am I still chasing bank transfers?
- Does it talk to my accounts software?
- Who's behind it, and how long have they been going?

## When a custom system makes more sense

Let's do the maths first, because it's the honest bit.

A 25-horse yard on LIVERYLive pays £50 a month, which is £600 a year. Most custom builds I do land between £2,000 and £5,000, which is somewhere between three and eight years of that. For a standard livery yard, off-the-shelf wins on cost, and it isn't close.

So when does custom make sense? When your yard doesn't look like the yard the software was designed for. Usually that's one of these.

**Services that don't fit a menu.** If what you sell is unusual, like rehab livery with exercise plans that change week to week, you can end up bending your business to fit the software.

**Arena hire to the public, with nobody on site.** Taking an arena booking online is common. Sending the rider a gate code automatically, so they can let themselves in at six in the morning while you're still asleep, is a different job. If that's what you want, ask each vendor whether it can talk to your gate system. If it can't, that's exactly the kind of gap a custom build fills.

**Livery, lessons and a shop under one roof.** Run all three and you can end up with three systems, three logins and three sets of customer records. One system that knows a livery is also a lesson client and a shop customer is a lot tidier.

**Something nobody else does.** Your own app for clients, a particular accounts setup, or kit you already own that needs connecting.

There's also a middle route: keep an off-the-shelf system for the livery admin and build only the bit it can't do. That's often the best value of all.

I said I'd be straight about this. I haven't built a livery system. Here's what I have built, and why it's relevant.

**[Fakenham Dog Field](/blog/building-fakenham-dog-field).** A private dog field behind a Paxton-controlled gate. Customers book and pay online through Stripe, and the system sends them a gate code that only works during their booking. The field runs with nobody on site. Swap "dog field" for "arena" and it's the same problem.

**[Horsebox Hire Software](/blog/building-horsebox-hire-software).** My own product. Online bookings with real-time availability, security deposits held on the card rather than charged, documents uploaded and checked, and hire agreements signed inside the booking. Every admin action is audit-logged. A yard taking outside bookings, or holding a deposit for temporary stabling, needs the same pieces. I've written more about [deposits and agreements](/blog/horsebox-hire-deposits-and-agreements) separately.

On the billing side, [Hoofflix](/blog/building-hoofflix) and The Balanced Horse both take recurring subscription payments every month, which is what livery fees are.

None of those is a livery system. But a livery system is mostly bookings, payments, recurring billing, records and controlling who can see what, and I've built all of those for real businesses.

## What it would cost

Every project gets a fixed quote before any work starts, and my [prices are published](/pricing).

- **A single piece**, like arena booking with payments and automatic gate codes, is a custom app. Those start at £1,299, and most land between £2,000 and £5,000.
- **A full yard system**, with livery billing, lessons, a shop and owner accounts, is a complex build: £5,000 and up.
- **Joining up what you already have**, like getting bookings into your accounts without retyping them, is automation. That starts at £799, and most jobs are £1,500 to £3,000.
- **Hosting** starts at £20 a month, and projects over £2,000 can be spread into monthly payments.

If one of the products above does what you need, use it. If you've tried them and your yard keeps not fitting, that's when it's worth a conversation.
