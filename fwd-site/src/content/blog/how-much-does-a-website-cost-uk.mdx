Most small business websites in the UK cost between £800 and £5,000 to build in 2026, and between £100 and £500 a year to keep running. You can get online for under £500 with a simple site, while agency builds usually start at around £5,000. Online shops, booking systems and anything custom cost more.

That's the short version. I get asked this more than any other question, and the honest answer really does depend on what you need. So here's exactly what it depends on, with real numbers, so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

## The Short Answer

Here's what you can expect to pay in the UK in 2026, depending on who builds it:

| Who builds it | Typical cost | Best for |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| You, on Wix or Squarespace | £15-£50 a month | Testing an idea, personal projects |
| Budget freelancer | £200-£500 | Very simple sites with minimal requirements |
| Professional freelancer | £800-£5,000 | Most small businesses |
| Agency | £5,000-£20,000 | Larger businesses with complex needs |
| Enterprise | £20,000+ | Large organisations, custom platforms |

Those numbers cover the build. Running costs come on top, and they're further down this page.

## Website Costs by Type of Site

The type of site matters more than who builds it. Here's what each one typically costs from a good freelancer:

| Type of site | Typical cost | What you get |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Starter site | £300-£800 | One to five pages, a contact form, set up for Google |
| Small business site | £800-£2,500 | Five to ten pages, custom design, a blog, proper SEO |
| Online shop | £2,000-£5,000 | Products, checkout, stock and order management |
| Booking system or customer portal | £2,500-£5,000 | Online bookings, payments, accounts, automatic emails |
| Web app or platform | £5,000+ | Multiple user types, subscriptions, custom logic |

Agency prices for the same kind of site are usually much higher, for reasons I'll come to.

## What Affects the Price

The gap between a £500 website and a £5,000 website isn't random. It comes down to a handful of things:

**Number of pages.** A single-page site takes a day or two. A 20-page site with different layouts and content types takes weeks. More pages means more design, more content and more testing.

**Custom design or a template.** A template where someone changes the colours and drops in your logo costs far less than a design built around your business. Both can look fine, but custom design takes much longer.

**What it needs to do.** A site that shows your services and has a contact form is straightforward. Add an online shop, bookings, customer accounts or payments and the work jumps.

**Connections to other systems.** Does the site need to talk to your CRM, your accounting software or a stock system? Each connection adds development time.

**Words and photos.** Some developers write your pages and source images. Most don't. If you need copywriting, budget for it or find a developer who includes it.

**Getting found on Google.** The basics should be included in every build: fast pages, sensible titles, a sitemap. Keyword research, content planning and ongoing work to climb the rankings cost extra.

## How Much Does an Online Shop Cost?

A small online shop typically costs £2,000 to £5,000 from a freelancer and £10,000 or more from an agency. The price depends on how many products you have, whether they come in sizes and colours, how you handle delivery, and whether the shop needs to talk to your stock or accounting system.

Running costs are higher than a normal website, too. You'll pay for the shop platform or hosting, card fees on every sale, and sometimes paid apps for things like reviews or subscriptions. Ask for those running costs in writing before you commit, not just the build price.

If you only sell a handful of things, you might not need a full shop at all. A simple payment link on a good website can do the job for a fraction of the cost.

## How Much Does a Website Redesign Cost?

A redesign usually costs about the same as a new website, and sometimes a little less if your content and page structure are still good. For a small business site, expect £800 to £3,000 from a freelancer.

The cheapest redesigns keep the words and replace the design. The most expensive ones change everything, and at that point it's really a new site. Either way, make sure your developer keeps your existing page addresses or redirects them properly. Get that wrong and you can lose the Google rankings you've already built up.

## How Much Does a Website Cost Per Year?

The build is a one-off. These aren't. For most small business sites, the yearly running cost looks like this:

| Running cost | Typical price | Notes |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Hosting | £5-£25 a month | More for shops, apps and busy sites |
| Domain name | £10-£15 a year | Register it in your own name |
| Security certificate (the padlock) | Free | Be wary of anyone charging for it |
| Business email | £0-£7 per person a month | Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 |
| Care plan | From £20 a month | Updates, backups, monitoring and small changes |
| Paid plugins (WordPress sites) | £100-£300 a year | Forms, security, backups |

Add it up and most small businesses spend £100 to £500 a year on a basic site, or more if someone looks after it for them.

**A real example:** my Professional website at £799, plus [hosting and maintenance](/services/hosting-maintenance) at £20 a month and a domain at around £12 a year, comes to roughly £1,050 for the first year and about £250 a year after that.

**Updates.** If you can't edit the site yourself, every text change or new page costs money. Small changes can be £30 to £50 each. Ask how you'll update your site before you commit.

## Why Are Some Developers So Cheap?

If someone quotes £200 for a website, it's worth knowing what you're getting.

Usually it's a WordPress theme with the colours changed, your logo dropped in and your text pasted into place. That can look fine at first glance, but these sites tend to be slow, full of code you don't need, and awkward to maintain. Watch out for:

- **No ongoing support.** Something breaks six months later and they've moved on.
- **Cookie-cutter design.** Your site looks like dozens of others.
- **Weak foundations for Google.** The site exists, but it's slow and hard for Google to understand.
- **Security problems.** Cheap WordPress builds with outdated plugins are a common target.

That said, low price alone isn't a red flag. Some people are just starting out and pricing low to build a portfolio. If their work is good and they reply quickly, they may be a bargain.

## Why Are Agencies So Expensive?

An agency has office rent, project managers, account managers, designers, developers and testers to pay, plus its own sales and marketing. All of that goes into every quote.

When an agency charges £10,000 for a website, the developer doing the work might spend the same time on it as a freelancer charging £3,000. The difference is overhead. For large projects that need a team working at once, an agency can be the right call. For most small business websites, you're paying for things you don't need. ## Questions to Ask Before You Pay

Whoever you choose, these questions will tell you a lot about a quote:

1. **Is the price fixed?** Or is it an estimate that can grow once work starts?
2. **Who owns the website and the domain?** It should be you, in writing.
3. **What does it cost to run after year one?** Hosting, care plans and renewals.
4. **Can I update it myself?** And if not, what does each change cost?
5. **What happens if something breaks?** Who fixes it, and how quickly?
6. **How will it help me get found on Google?** Listen for specifics, not promises of page one.
7. **Can I see sites you've built?** Then check they load quickly on your phone.
